1.2 Infinite structured carrier

4. Direction (c): corrected gcd correspondence
Theorem T7A - Phase registry hears the gcd
Let
                                                        μ_n=z in U(1):z^n=1.

For positive integers n_1,n_2 and g=gcd(n_1,n_2),
                                                         μ_n_1 ∩ μ_n_2=μ_g.

Consequently there are exactly g oriented common phases.
Proof. If z in μ_n_1 ∩ μ_n_2, then the order of z divides both n_1 and n_2, hence divides g. Thus z in μ_g. Conversely, every
g-th root of unity is both an n_1-th and an n_2-th root because g divides n_1,n_2. Therefore the intersection is exactly μ_g,
which has g elements. QED.
This is the operator-free TUFT checksum. It is a statement about the phase registry itself.

6. What "energy" means in this edition
Theorems T7A-T7C require no physical energy. T7A is a group-theoretic registry statement. T7B and T7C concern the
dimensionless spectrum of a declared combinatorial operator.
To interpret the spectral values as physical energies, an additional operator model and calibration must be supplied. In the
terminology of the Hamiltonian calibration supplement:
                          registry organizes, operator interrogates, calibration measures.

This edition therefore uses "energy as residue" as a Tier II, operator-relative reading. It does not claim that the operator-free
registry creates energy, selects a Hamiltonian, or supplies SI scale.
Nor is "residue" used here as an unqualified substitute for every invariant. A formal residue claim in the obstruction calculus
must name its representation class and obstruction functional. The exact Tier I content of this paper is the phase-intersection
theorem, the inversion quotient, the corrected counting formulas, and the conditional perturbation statement.
