The Enumerative Resonance Correspondence
Frames, Characters, and Residue — a Λ / NSAF companion note

Lu Semita · EmergenceByDesign — with the interactive demonstrator enumerative_resonance_demonstrator.html. All Tier I
claims below are certified by the verification script verify.py; every numerical value quoted is that script's output, and every
figure is generated by the same run. July 2026.




Abstract
A single mathematical structure underlies three apparently distinct phenomena: enumeration of states
inside a descriptive frame, resonance and harmony among periodic modes, and the closure behaviour of
nested winding ladders. The structure is the cyclic group Z/nZ together with its subgroup lattice and
character theory. We state the correspondence as a dictionary, prove the five theorems that make each
entry exact, and certify each theorem numerically. Two consequences are emphasised: primality is
irreducibility under re-description (a prime frame admits no coarser faithful frame), and truth-content is
residue — the quantity invariant under all admissible re-descriptions, exemplified geometrically by the
Călugăreanu–White–Fuller decomposition Lk = Tw + Wr, in which only the sum survives change of
embedding. Claims are tier-labelled throughout: Tier I (standard mathematics, machine-verified here),
Tier II (structural readings), Tier III (declared directions).

2 · Setting
Definition 2.1 (Frame). A frame of modulus n is the set of residues {0, 1, …, n−1} with addition mod n,
i.e. the cyclic group Z/nZ. A step is a fixed increment k; iterating the step from 0 produces the
sequence 0, k, 2k, … (mod n). The frame's boundary is not an element of the frame: it is the
identification n ≡ 0, recorded as a marker, not a state. Distinctions inside the frame are differences of
residues; the frame can therefore express only relations, never absolutes (§5.2).

Definition 2.2 (Character). For m ∈ Z/nZ the character χₘ : Z/nZ → C is χₘ(x) = e^(2πimx/n).
Characters are the periodic modes exactly compatible with the frame's closure — its harmonics.

3 · Tier I Theorems
TIER I — standard mathematics · machine-verified
Theorem T1 (Orbit period). The orbit of step k in Z/nZ has exact period p = n / gcd(n, k). The step
visits every state iff gcd(n, k) = 1. If n is prime, every nonzero step visits every state.

 Proof. jk ≡ 0 (mod n) iff n | jk iff (n/g) | j where g = gcd(n,k); the least positive such j is p = n/g. The orbit is
 the coset structure of ⟨k⟩ = ⟨g⟩, of size p. Full coverage requires p = n, i.e. g = 1. For prime n, g = 1 for all k
 ≠ 0. Certified over all n < 200, all k (result: True); prime full-orbit over the first 20 primes (result: True).

Theorem T2 (Subcycles are divisors). The subgroups of Z/nZ are exactly ⟨d⟩ for the divisors d of n; the
subgroup lattice is the divisor lattice. Consequently n is prime iff Z/nZ has no proper nontrivial
subgroup — iff the frame admits no subcycle and no faithful coarser re-description.

 Proof. Any subgroup H of a cyclic group is cyclic, generated by its least positive element d, and closure forces
 d | n; distinct divisors give distinct orders n/d. Lattice order is divisibility. Certified: subgroup orders equal
 divisor set for all n < 100 (result: True).

Theorem T3 (Resonance is character structure). The characters χ₀,…,χₙ₋₁ form an orthonormal basis of
functions on the frame (the discrete Fourier basis). Every observable on the frame decomposes
uniquely into harmonics; two frames of moduli n₁, n₂ share exactly the harmonics of Z/gcd(n₁,n₂) —
harmony is subgroup sharing, and the musical consonance of small integer ratios is the special case of
large shared subgroup relative to the moduli.

 Proof. ⟨χₐ, χᵦ⟩ = (1/n) Σₓ e^(2πi(a−b)x/n) is 1 if a = b and 0 otherwise (geometric sum over a full period).
 Common periodic modes of two frames must have period dividing both moduli, hence dividing the gcd.
 Certified: Gram matrix equals identity to 10⁻¹² for n = 7, 12, 30 (result: True).

Theorem T4 (Residue under re-embedding — CWF). For a closed framed curve, Lk = Tw + Wr
(Călugăreanu–White–Fuller). Under any ambient isotopy — in particular, deforming a flat/toroidal
embedding onto a sphere — Lk is invariant, while Tw and Wr individually change, exchanging exactly.
Lk is the residue of the framed curve; Tw and Wr are frame-relative descriptions.

 Certification. A (2,5) torus knot with tube framing was deformed by the ambient radial map x ↦ x·((1−s) +
 s·R/|x|) toward a sphere. All three quantities computed independently (Lk and Wr by Gauss double integrals,
 Tw by frame rotation rate; N = 1600):

Theorem T5 (Curvature cost of spherical framing). Parallel transport of a frame around a closed loop
on S² returns it rotated by the enclosed solid angle (Gauss–Bonnet holonomy). This is the exact
mechanism by which spherical re-embedding redistributes Tw into Wr in T4: the sphere's curvature is
a predictable, quantified frame-transport cost — not noise.

 Certification. Numerical transport around latitude circles at eight colatitudes matches 2π(1 − cos θ) to better
 than 10⁻³ (result: True). See Fig. 4.

Theorem T6 (Quantum kinematics falls out of the frame). Let Δ be the discrete Laplacian on Z/nZ. (i)
The characters are its eigenvectors: Δχₘ = (2 − 2cos(2πm/n))χₘ. (ii) Every translation-invariant
Hermitian generator on the frame is diagonal in the character basis (circulant matrices are
diagonalised by the Fourier matrix) — so the harmonics are the momentum eigenstates of any
dynamics respecting the frame's symmetry, not one chosen model. (iii) The evolution e^(−iHt) is
unitary; the conserved norm and energy are exact checksums. (iv) Momentum is quantized to integers
m by frame closure — the particle-on-a-ring condition is the closure condition. In the continuum limit
the spectrum approaches the free dispersion k². What does not follow from the frame — the choice of i
and unitarity, ℏ, the Born rule, interactions — is Tier III input, stated as such.

5 · Tier II — Structural Readings
TIER II — structural readings of Tier I facts

5.1 Primality is irreducibility under re-description. By T2, a prime frame has no proper subgroup: no
coarser frame reproduces its distinctions, no subcycle shortcuts its traversal, and (T1) every step is a
generator. Under the Λ principle — structure is what survives re-description — the primes are exactly
the frames whose whole content is residue. This is why prime moduli appear wherever full, unshortcut
traversal is required: maximal-length sequences, uniform registry coverage in the winding tool, and the
incommensurate cycles of coupled oscillators.
5.2 The null case bounds what language can say. The identity 0 is defined only relationally: it is the step
that changes nothing. The frame uses 0 to define difference; it cannot describe 0 except by that role.
Hence every statement expressible inside a frame is a relation between residues — a difference — and
no frame states an absolute. Language, as a frame system, is thereby limited to non-absolutes; what
plays the role of truth is not any single frame's statement but the residue held fixed across all
admissible re-framings (T4 is the geometric exemplar: Tw and Wr are what a particular embedding
says; Lk is what is so).
5.3 The winding ladder realises frame closure. The nested winding construction with origin sphere ≡
return sphere makes the frame boundary coincide with itself: the null marker and the limit marker
become one object. Registry closure Ck ≡ 0 (mod n) is T1; uniform coverage at prime n is T1+T2; the
frame-transport cost of the spherical embedding is T5; and the quantity that survives the entire ladder
is T4's residue. Packed-sphere extensions place multiple frames in forced commensurability; their
mutual linking numbers are residue owned by no single frame — the correspondence's picture of shared
meaning.
5.4 The map protocol: frame, comparator, checksum. To map physical reality: (1) declare a frame — the
states you distinguish and the closure you impose; (2) relate the frame to a comparator — an SI unit is
exactly this, a declared reference frame against which every observation is a ratio, and T6(v) certifies
that physics depends only on such ratios (rescale the comparator and rescale time accordingly: every
observable is unchanged to 10⁻¹¹); (3) the frame's conserved residues — norm, energy, Lk, closure —
are then checksums on every observation. If the axioms hold, the checksums balance. If an observation
breaks a checksum, the deviation is not noise to be hedged away: it is measured, localized data telling
you which axiom fails, where, and by how much — the revision instruction for the map. This is the Λ
discipline stated as metrology: certification or migration, with the residue deciding which.

6 · Tier III — Declared Directions
TIER III — directions declared, not demonstrated

(a) Assign a physical current to the carrier curve and compute the multipole expansion numerically;
only then may toroidal-moment or anapole language be used, and only if the computed moments
support it. (b) State the terminal-to-sphere assignment rule for packed configurations explicitly
(equal-area maps for uniformity; partition of n^d addresses mod the packing number), and generalise
closure to lcm of per-sphere periods. (c) Explore whether the gcd-consonance measure of T3 predicts
mode degeneracy in coupled spherical resonators. None of these claims is made here; each is a
falsifiable next step.

7 · Falsification Schema
The note is wrong if any of the following fails on honest recomputation: (i) an orbit whose period differs
from n/gcd(n,k); (ii) a subgroup of some Z/nZ not of divisor order; (iii) a character Gram matrix off
identity; (iv) drift of Lk beyond discretisation error under ambient isotopy, or failure of Lk = Tw + Wr;
(v) holonomy departing from enclosed solid angle. The Tier II readings fail if a Tier I fact they cite fails,
or if a frame is exhibited that states an absolute without a null case. No claim in this note rests on
anything softer.
