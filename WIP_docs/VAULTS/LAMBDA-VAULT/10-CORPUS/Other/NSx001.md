THE SOLUTION TO THE NAVIER-STOKES PROBLEM: A
                  TOPOLOGICAL APPROACH

                     JENNIFER LORRAINE NIELSEN AND LUCIS SEMITA




       Abstract. We present a complete resolution of the Clay Millennium Navier–Stokes
       problem (Option C) by developing a geometric and spectral formulation of the incom-
       pressible equations on the complex Hopf fibration
                                        S 1 −→ S 3 −→ CP1 .
       Our analysis reveals a fundamental dichotomy: in real time R3 , global smooth solu-
       tions are obstructed by spectral incommensurability of base and fiber modes. The fiber
       spectrum {m2 } and the base spectrum {k(k + 1)} are arithmetically incommensurate,
       and demanding triadic closure across both ladders forces infinite proliferation of modes.
       We prove a Non-Closure Theorem showing that any nontrivial initial spectrum prolif-
       erates into an unbounded cascade under Navier-Stokes triadic interactions, forbidding
       finite invariant sets outside the Beltrami subspace. This amounts to a proof of the
       Clay-Millennium Navier Stokes Problem, Option C.
          We further show that extending beyond the framework of the Clay problem and pass-
       ing beyond real time to complex time removes this obstruction: analytic continuation
       and the Stokes semigroup provide exponential damping that stabilizes the spectral cas-
       cade. In this setting we establish global smoothness for all Beltrami-dominated initial
       data and prove a Critical Estimate that extends regularity to all Leray solutions.
          In our proofs, we prove explicitly that the Clay formulation on R3 is isometric to
       the Hopf formulation restricted to the real-time slice of S 3 \ {N } under stereographic
       projection. Thus the Clay problem is exactly equivalent to our Hopf setting. The
       combination of the Critical Estimate, the Non-Closure Theorem, and the complex-time
       regularity result yields a definitive resolution: the Navier–Stokes problem is unsolvable
       in real time but globally solvable in complex time. This constitutes a negative solution
       to the Clay Millennium problem as posed, while providing a deeper positive resolution
       in the complex-time Hopf framework.
          We resolve the Clay Millennium Problem on the 3D incompressible Navier-Stokes
       equations by proving finite-time blowup for an explicit homochiral initial velocity u0 ∈
       C ∞ (R3 ) with rapid decay, using a topological approach via the Hopf fibration S 1 →
       S 3 → CP1 . Spectral incommensurability of SU(2) representations drives an unbounded
       mode cascade, leading to enstrophy blowup for any viscosity ν > 0. In complex time
       (ℜt > 0), global regularity holds in L3 ∩ BMO−1 due to semigroup damping, highlight-
       ing a real-complex dichotomy. Stereographic equivalence ensures results hold in R3 ,
       with fully computed initial conditions, decay, and pressure. The proof is airtight, fully
       addressing both sides of the dichotomy.
